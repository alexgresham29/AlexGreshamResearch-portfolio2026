About This Portfolio

This site serves as a living record of the technical skills and research findings I have developed during the Summer 2026 intensive program. It is organized into two primary sections: an R Programming section documenting my growth in statistical computing and data visualization, and a Lab Research section presenting a formal account of experimental work performed at the Gresham Laboratory at NYU.

All R analyses were conducted in RStudio using R Markdown notebooks. Laboratory experiments were performed under the supervision of the Gresham Lab and are recorded in chronological order from May–June 2026. This portfolio will continue to be updated as research progresses.

Research Overview

The central aim of the laboratory component of this program is to develop and validate molecular tools for genetic manipulation of S. cerevisiae, a powerful model organism widely used in studies of cell biology, genetics, and evolutionary genomics. My work has focused on the construction, verification, and transformation of recombinant plasmids, as well as the phenotypic characterization of yeast knockout strains through growth assays.

Key techniques employed include alkaline lysis minipreparation of plasmid DNA, restriction endonuclease digestion and agarose gel electrophoresis, polymerase chain reaction (PCR), Sanger sequencing, and high-efficiency lithium acetate yeast transformation. Growth phenotype data were collected using a Tecan 96-well plate reader and analyzed computationally using the Growthcurver R package.
